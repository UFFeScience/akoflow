Getting started / Desktop / Experiments

AkôFlow 1.0.12 brings MCP to Claude and Codex

AkôFlow 1.0.12 introduces MCP integration. Connect Claude or Codex to create, execute and inspect scientific workflows, with a hands-on example.

Read the story ↓
Wesley Ferreira (@ovvesley), Boosted with LLMs
November 4, 2026 · 13 min read

Starting with version 1.0.12, AkôFlow includes MCP integration to connect AI apps such as Claude and Codex directly to its workflow engine. You can now describe an experiment in a conversation and ask your assistant to create workflow definitions, prepare execution plans, start runs and inspect their results through AkôFlow tools.

MCP—Model Context Protocol—provides that connection. The assistant works with the same persisted definitions, plans and runs that you see in Desktop, using the permissions you grant to its connection. The 1.0.12 release brings managed app connections, native client setup, connection verification, credential rotation and revocation.

This tutorial takes that connection from setup to a result you can inspect: three activities turn the measurements 2, 4 and 6 into a report containing Mean: 4.00. You will connect your AI app, give it focused prompts, inspect the saved DAG, and execute the workflow on a local Docker runtime.

Montage 050: a sky made of workflows

Read the story ↓
Wesley Ferreira (@ovvesley), Boosted with LLMs
October 21, 2026 · 19 min read

The stars in the opening image are the output of a scientific computation. Before the color mosaic existed, individual observations had to be reprojected, their overlaps compared, their backgrounds reconciled, and their pixels combined. AkôFlow coordinates those steps as a workflow and keeps the execution evidence alongside the result.

This field note follows Montage 050, a 58-activity astronomy workflow. We will look at what the activities do, how to reproduce the local example, and what an existing completed experiment tells us about planning and execution.
